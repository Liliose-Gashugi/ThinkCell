Journey

Empowering women in Rwanda by bridging the mental health gap through education, community, and lasting change.

Mission Statement

Our mission is to empower women from Rwanda, by closing gender gaps in healthcare through education and community-driven change, creating opportunities and role models that inspire trust, and believe in women's abilities.

We envision a Rwanda where every woman has access to quality mental health care, where awareness replaces stigma, where communities support healing, and where women lead the transformation of healthcare from the ground up.

Rwanda Mental Health Survey, 2018

Although Rwanda has made significant progress in strengthening its healthcare system, access to quality services, particularly mental health care, in areas like Huye, in the southern part of Rwanda, remains limited.

According to the Rwanda Mental Health Survey (2018), approximately 20–23% of the population experiences a mental disorder, with women mostly affected (23.2% compared to 16.6% of men). A shortage of specialized professionals and limited availability of services, especially in rural areas, leaves many individuals without adequate care, negatively impacting productivity, family stability, and overall well-being.

In summary: Low awareness and affordability of mental health services in Rwanda, especially among women.

Solution

Our solution tackles low mental health awareness and stigma among women in Rwanda through three cost-effective channels. Social media Awareness uses TikTok and Instagram, with Rwandan influencer collaborations, to educate audiences about mental health. Community Outreach brings talks into schools and universities to normalize mental health conversations and encourages youth-led mental health clubs. A Live Chat Box lets users ask questions, get recommended steps, and receive referrals to clinics and practitioners.

Our team implements this alongside mental health practitioners. It benefits women seeking mental health support, partner practitioners, and the team through paid partnerships and revenue. While call centers cost $5,000–$50,000, our model runs at just $100–$200 — with the power to reach global audiences through trending hashtags.

Six transformative E-LAB challenges that shaped our thinking and brought us closer to real impact.

02

Challenge Two — Community Story

In Challenge Two, our team chose to tell the story of the Ashanti tribe in Ghana. We highlighted the rich culture, traditions, and strengths of the Ashanti people, while also addressing common misconceptions about their community. Each team member took on a specific role to creatively present the story. Through this challenge, we learned the importance of storytelling in preserving and celebrating African heritage, and how understanding diverse communities can inform the way we approach our own work in Rwanda.

03

Challenge Three — Community Impact

In Challenge Three, our team visited Enfants de Dieu, a school that supports children from low-income families. We engaged with the students and staff to understand the challenges these communities face, particularly around health and wellbeing. Through this visit, we saw firsthand how limited resources affect vulnerable families and how mental health often goes unaddressed in these settings. The experience reinforced our commitment to making mental health care more accessible and reminded us that meaningful change starts at the community level. It also showed us how small, direct actions — like showing up and listening — can make a real difference in people's lives

04

Challenge Four — Business Visit

In Challenge Four, our team interviewed Nicolas, a mental health practitioner, to learn about his work, the challenges he faces, and the state of mental health care in Rwanda. He shared insights on the barriers women encounter when seeking help — including stigma, limited access to services, and affordability. Nicolas explained how the shortage of mental health professionals in rural areas like Huye leaves many women without support. His firsthand experience deepened our understanding of the problem and directly inspired our solution. This conversation also led to a potential partnership, with Nicolas becoming a key collaborator in our project.

05

Challenge Five — Grant Pitch

This challenge was about competing for a $10,000 grant by developing a project proposal and a 3-minute video pitch. The goal was to present our Think Tank's mission, the problem we identified, our proposed solution, and the impact we aim to create — showing why our project deserves the grant.
